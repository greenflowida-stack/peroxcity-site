The Process

Mix it, spray it, let the oxygen work.

PeroXcity ships as a two-part concentrate kit: Part A (35% hydrogen peroxide) and Part B Accelerator. Mix with water on site, and the reaction starts the moment it hits the surface.

01

Mix

Order matters. Add Part A to a clean plastic container and dilute it with clean water first to your target ratio — 1:4, 1:3, or 1:2 depending on the job. Only then add Part B Accelerator per the chart and stir or agitate for about a minute. Never add Part B to undiluted concentrate. Avoid well water.

See mix ratio guide
02

Spray

Apply generously and evenly to a dry surface using a plastic sprayer — never metal. Start with a 10′×10′ test area. Oxygen creates foam as it reacts with contamination.

Plastic sprayer only
03

Wait

Allow 15 to 45 minutes of contact time — it depends on temperature, sun, and humidity, so give it longer on cool or humid days. A stiff brush or broom ensures uniform contact on stubborn spots if needed; harsh scrubbing isn't required. Keep the surface wet with foam and avoid direct sunlight where possible.

15–45 min dwell
04

Rinse

Rinse with light pressure — a hose or a pressure washer on a low setting is all it takes to lift the loosened grime away. Work in small sections until the surface is clean. PeroXcity keeps working in the pores of the surface even after you've moved on.

Light pressure rinse

Wear protective gloves, clothing, and eye/face protection. If on skin or in eyes: rinse immediately with plenty of water for several minutes and seek medical attention. Aluminum & painted surfaces. Avoid contact with bare aluminum and with inexpensive aluminum gutters (store-bought or budget-installed). These carry very little protective coating, and the oxygen reaction can react with the exposed aluminum underneath. Always test any painted surface in a small, hidden spot before applying. FAQ

Questions we hear a lot.

Everything from dilution to dwell time. Can't find what you need? Reach out and we'll get you an answer.

Is there a version I don't have to mix?
Yes — the 7% Ready-To-Use gallon comes pre-mixed and already activated. No Part B, no measuring: pour it into a plastic pump sprayer and start. It comes in a 1-gallon jug or a 5-gallon pail. It's the easiest way to try PeroXcity, and it's the version headed to Amazon. Pros doing volume work will still get more cleaning per dollar from the concentrate kit.
What makes PeroXcity different from a regular cleaner?
It's built on hydrogen peroxide instead of bleach or detergents, and it ships as a concentrate you mix on site. The oxygen reaction lifts mold, mildew, grease, and stains on contact, then breaks down into water and oxygen — no harsh residue left behind. Because you set the ratio, one kit covers everything from a light house wash to a restaurant hood.
Do I need to dilute it before use?
Yes — PeroXcity is a two-part concentrate, and the order matters. Add Part A to a clean plastic container and dilute it with clean water first to your target ratio (1:4 light/everyday, 1:3 heavy organic, 1:2 super strength). Only then add Part B Accelerator per the chart and stir for about a minute. Never add Part B to undiluted concentrate. A 1-gallon kit makes up to 5 gallons at 1:4. Not sure which ratio? Start lower and adjust.
What is Part B Accelerator, and how much do I add?
Part B is the activator that kicks off the oxygen reaction. Add it last, to the already-diluted mix (Part A + water) — never to undiluted concentrate: 6 oz per gallon at 1:4, 8 oz at 1:3, and 12 oz at 1:2. One full 32 oz bottle treats a 5-gallon batch; two bottles for 10 gallons. Use the Part B chart in the Mix Ratio Guide, or the PeroXcity Mix calculator, for other batch sizes.
What kind of sprayer should I use?
Plastic only — never metal. The mix can be corrosive to raw metals over time. It's safe on stainless steel and most coated metals as a surface being cleaned, but the sprayer itself should be plastic. Test a small area first on any metal surface.
How long does a mixed batch stay good?
Once mixed with water and Part B, PeroXcity stays active for months. Use clean water — avoid well water — since better-quality water helps prolong strength. Keep it in a sealed plastic container out of direct sun.
What surfaces can I use it on?
Wood, masonry, pavers, siding, and roofing (including TPO membranes), pool cages and screen enclosures, fences, marine and waterfront structures, RVs and boats, fabric and awnings, and commercial kitchen surfaces like hoods, grease traps, tile grout, and stainless steel. See the "Where To Use It" section above for the full breakdown.
How long does it take to work?
Contact time is 15 to 45 minutes depending on temperature, sun, and humidity — warm, dry conditions work faster; cool or humid days need the longer end. Apply generously to a dry surface, keep it wet with foam during the dwell time, then rinse with light pressure (a hose or a low pressure-washer setting). PeroXcity keeps working in the pores of the surface even after you've moved on.
Do I need to scrub or pressure wash?
No. The oxygen reaction does the lifting on its own. If a stubborn area needs a little extra help after the dwell time, a stiff brush or broom is usually enough — a light-pressure rinse is all it takes.
Is it safe to use around pets, kids, and plants?
PeroXcity is formulated to be low odor and low toxicity, a gentler alternative to chlorine bleach cleaners. That said, the concentrated product itself should be kept out of reach and handled per the label — always keep people, pets, and plants clear of the area until it's been rinsed.
Is it safe to use near water, like docks or marinas?
Yes — it's formulated to be safe for aquatic environments, which is why it's a fit for docks, boat slips, and other waterfront structures where fish, plants, and water quality matter.
What does it break down into?
Water and oxygen. That's the whole byproduct — no phosphates, no bleach, no harsh chemical residue left on the surfaces you clean.
What sizes are available?
The standard 1-gallon Concentrate Kit (Part A 35% + Part B Accelerator) makes up to 5 gallons of finished product. Part B Accelerator comes in the kit. There is also a 7% Ready-To-Use in 1- and 5-gallon sizes that needs no mixing. For fleet and distributor accounts, 55-gallon drums and 275-gallon totes ship at 7% hydrogen peroxide with accelerator packed separately — not ready-to-use, so you activate on site.
What safety precautions should I take?
Both parts carry a DANGER hazard label. Part A (35% hydrogen peroxide) is an oxidizer that can intensify fire and causes severe skin burns and eye damage; Part B Accelerator (45%) causes severe skin burns and eye damage and may be corrosive to metals. Wear chemical-resistant gloves, protective clothing, and safety goggles. If on skin or in eyes, rinse with plenty of water for several minutes and seek medical attention. If swallowed, rinse mouth, don't induce vomiting, and call a Poison Center. Keep out of reach of children, store Part A in its original vented container, and read the label and SDS before use. Avoid bare aluminum and cheap aluminum gutters, and always test painted surfaces in a hidden spot first — badly prepped paint can blister. Questions: 1-888-253-6962.
