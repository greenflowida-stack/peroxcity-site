## Why PeroXcity beats bleach (the core pitch)
- **It cleans with oxygen, not chlorine.** Hydrogen peroxide releases oxygen that breaks apart organic matter — mold, mildew, algae, moss, grease, pet and biological odors — rather than just bleaching the color out of it.
- **It keeps working.** Once mixed it stays active for months, and it keeps working in the pores of the surface after you rinse. Bleach stops the moment it dries.
- **It breaks down into water and oxygen.** Nothing is left behind. Bleach leaves chlorine residue and salts that can damage plants, corrode metal fasteners, and have to be neutralized.
- **Safer around landscaping, pets, docks, and boats.** Bleach runoff kills grass and shrubs and is toxic to aquatic life; PeroXcity runoff is water and oxygen.
- **No chlorine fumes.** Bleach gives off chlorine gas, and is dangerous when mixed with other cleaners (ammonia, acids). PeroXcity has no chlorine.
- **Doesn't attack the surface.** Bleach degrades asphalt shingle granules, roofing warranties often forbid it, and it weakens wood fibers and fabrics. PeroXcity lifts the growth without stripping the surface.
- **No lingering smell.** No bleach odor on the deck, in the house, or in the truck.
- **Truly deodorizes.** Oxygen destroys the organic source of odors (urine, mildew, grease, smoke) instead of masking it.

## How it works — the process
1. **Mix (order matters).** Add Part A to a clean plastic container, **dilute with clean water first** to the target ratio, and **only then** add Part B Accelerator per the chart. Stir or agitate about a minute. **Never add Part B to undiluted concentrate.** Avoid well water. Ready-To-Use needs no mixing.
2. **Spray** generously and evenly onto a dry surface with a **plastic** sprayer — never metal. Start with a 10 ft × 10 ft test area. Oxygen foam forms where it reacts with contamination.
3. **Wait 15–45 minutes.** Depends on temperature, sun, and humidity: warm, dry conditions work faster; cool or humid days need the longer end. Keep the surface wet with foam; avoid direct sun where possible. A stiff brush or broom helps on stubborn spots; harsh scrubbing isn't needed.
4. **Rinse with light pressure** — a garden hose or a pressure washer on a low setting. Work in small sections. It keeps working in the pores afterward.

## Where NOT to use it / cautions
- **Galvanized metal and bare aluminum** — avoid, including inexpensive aluminum gutters. The oxygen reaction can react with exposed aluminum.
- **Painted surfaces** — always test a small hidden spot first. A poorly prepped paint job can trap organic matter between coats and blister when the cleaner reacts with it.
- Use plastic sprayers and containers only, never metal.
- Don't mix with other chemicals.

## Safety (say this exactly)
- Part A (35% hydrogen peroxide) is an oxidizer: can intensify fire; causes severe skin burns and eye damage.
