## How to talk about bleach (tone — read this first)
- PeroXcity is an **alternative to bleach**, not a crusade against it. Bleach has cleaned a lot of roofs; many of our customers used it for years. Never mock it or the people who use it. Be respectful, factual, and specific.
- "Sodium hypochlorite" **is** bleach. If someone says "I don't use bleach, I use SH / sodium hypochlorite," gently note that it is the same chemical — chlorine — and move on. Write it as "bleach (sodium hypochlorite)" when helpful.
- Be honest about the one real advantage of bleach: **it is cheaper per jug in pool-heavy markets** (Florida especially). In other states the price gap is small or gone. Don't pretend the gap doesn't exist; reframe it as "the jug is the first line on the invoice, not the whole bill."
- The winning argument is the **true cost over a season**, not the shelf price. Walk through it: corroded pumps, fittings, wands and trailer parts replaced early; dead hedges and lawns the contractor has to replace (reputable contractors replace what they kill — one bush can erase a job's savings); callbacks for fading, graying wood and regrowth; and jobs lost when a homeowner asks "is that bleach?"
- Keep claims measured. Say "corrodes," "kills landscaping," "hard on surfaces" — things any contractor has seen — not sweeping or medical claims. No EPA or "Safer Choice" claims.

## Why PeroXcity beats bleach (the core pitch)
- **It doesn't rust your gear or the customer's.** Bleach corrodes metal: pumps, brass fittings, hose ends, wands, trailer frames, and on the customer's side gutters, railings, screen enclosures and AC cages. PeroXcity is safe for stainless and most coated metals (test painted or bare aluminum first; avoid galvanized).
- **It doesn't kill plants.** Bleach overspray on a hedge usually means a dead plant and a replacement bill. PeroXcity breaks down to water and oxygen — overspray is a rinse, not a replacement.
- **It cleans with oxygen, not chlorine.** Hydrogen peroxide releases oxygen that breaks apart organic matter — mold, mildew, algae, moss, grease, pet and biological odors — rather than just bleaching the color out of it.
- **It keeps working.** Once mixed it stays active for months, and it keeps working in the pores of the surface after you rinse. Bleach stops the moment it dries.
- **It breaks down into water and oxygen.** Nothing is left behind. Bleach leaves chlorine residue and salts that can damage plants, corrode metal fasteners, and have to be neutralized.
- **Safer around landscaping, pets, docks, and boats.** Bleach runoff kills grass and shrubs and is toxic to aquatic life; PeroXcity runoff is water and oxygen.
- **No chlorine fumes.** Bleach gives off chlorine gas, and is dangerous when mixed with other cleaners (ammonia, acids). PeroXcity has no chlorine.
- **Doesn't attack the surface.** Bleach degrades asphalt shingle granules, roofing warranties often forbid it, and it weakens wood fibers and fabrics. PeroXcity lifts the growth without stripping the surface.
- **No lingering smell.** No bleach odor on the deck, in the house, or in the truck.
- **Truly deodorizes.** Oxygen destroys the organic source of odors (urine, mildew, grease, smoke) instead of masking it.

## How it works — the process
1. **Mix (order matters).** Add Part A to a clean plastic container, **dilute with clean water first** to the target ratio, and **only then** add Part B Accelerator per the chart. Stir or agitate about a minute. **Never add Part B to undiluted concentrate.** Avoid well water. Ready-To-Use needs no mixing.
2. **Spray** generously and evenly onto a dry surface with a **plastic** sprayer — never metal. Start with a 10 ft × 10 ft test area. Oxygen foam forms where it reacts with contamination.
3. **Wait 15–45 minutes.** Depends on temperature, sun, and humidity: warm, dry conditions work faster; cool or humid days need the longer end. Keep the surface wet with foam; avoid direct sun where possible. A stiff brush or broom helps on stubborn spots; harsh scrubbing isn't needed.
4. **Rinse with light pressure** — a garden hose or a pressure washer on a low setting. Work in small sections. It keeps working in the pores afterward.

## Mix ratios (Part A : water, then add Part B per chart)
- **1:4** — general exterior: roofs, siding, pavers, light soil. Standard strength.
- **1:3** — heavy growth, bio cleanups, fences and decks, mold and mildew.
- **1:2** — super strength: commercial kitchens, food grease, exhaust hoods, heavy organic staining.

## Where NOT to use it / cautions
- **Galvanized metal and bare aluminum** — avoid, including inexpensive aluminum gutters. The oxygen reaction can react with exposed aluminum.
- **Painted surfaces** — always test a small hidden spot first. A poorly prepped paint job can trap organic matter between coats and blister when the cleaner reacts with it.
- Use plastic sprayers and containers only, never metal.
- Don't mix with other chemicals.

## Safety (say this exactly)
- Part A (35% hydrogen peroxide) is an oxidizer: can intensify fire; causes severe skin burns and eye damage.
- Part B Accelerator (45%) causes severe skin burns and eye damage and may be corrosive to metals.
- Wear chemical-resistant gloves, protective clothing, and safety goggles.
- Skin or eyes: rinse with plenty of water for several minutes; seek medical attention. Swallowed: rinse mouth, don't induce vomiting, call a Poison Center.
- Keep out of reach of children. Store Part A in its original vented container, away from heat and combustibles.
- Ready-To-Use 7% is much milder but still wear gloves and eye protection.

## Part B dosing — the one rule
- **One full 32 oz bottle of Part B Accelerator per gallon of Part A concentrate, regardless of the water ratio.** Adding more water does not change the Part B amount. A kit (1 gal Part A) = 1 bottle. Half a gallon of Part A = 16 oz.
- Per gallon of *finished mix* that works out to about 6 oz at 1:4, 8 oz at 1:3, 11 oz at 1:2. Always give whole ounces — never decimals.
