Why we don't use bleach. The documents.

Bleach (sodium hypochlorite) is what most exterior cleaners use, and it works. These are the reasons we switched, with the exact words from the people who wrote them — poison centers, NIOSH, the Chlorine Institute, roofing manufacturers, and Florida and California cities. Every line links to the original document.

It's on the label

The bleach label itself says it's toxic to fish and can't go in the water.
"This pesticide is toxic to fish and aquatic organisms." "Do not discharge effluent containing this product into lakes, streams, ponds, estuaries, oceans or waters."
EPA-registered label12.5% sodium hypochlorite product label (EPA)
Professional-strength bleach is labeled DANGER: corrosive, irreversible eye damage.
"DANGER: Corrosive. Causes irreversible eye damage and skin burns. Harmful if swallowed, inhaled, or absorbed through skin."
EPA-registered labelsame label

Your equipment

The chlorine industry's own manual says keep bleach away from almost every metal, stainless included.
"As a general rule, do not allow metals (except for titanium and tantalum) to come in contact with sodium hypochlorite." "Virtually all common metals such as mild steel, stainless steel and Hastalloys will corrode rapidly."
Industry manualChlorine Institute, Sodium Hypochlorite Manual (Pamphlet 96)
Pump makers rate bleach poorly against stainless and rubber; peroxide rates well against stainless.
Bleach <20%: duplex stainless "B", Buna-N "not recommended." Hydrogen peroxide <30%: duplex stainless "A."
ManufacturerAmpco Pumps material compatibility table
Pump makers and the suppliers who sell soft-wash gear say the same thing: bleach eats pumps.
"Cleaning chemicals are highly corrosive and can easily damage diaphragm pumps." — "Sodium Hypochlorite is extremely corrosive. It will slowly deteriorate the inside of your pump as it passes through."
Manufacturer / supplierPumptec · PowerWash.com

The customer's plants and property

University horticulturists warn that bleach washing harms landscape plants.
"Chlorine bleach can be harmful to landscape plants." "Do not allow the solution to dry on the plants, wash it off immediately."
University extensionUniversity of Maryland Extension
Roofing manufacturers tell bleach users to cover every plant under the roof first.
"Prior to applying the solution, all ground vegetation directly under the roof should be covered."
ManufacturerOwens Corning technical bulletin on algae
The roofing industry itself calls bleach cleaning temporary.
"The effectiveness of such cleaning is only temporary, and the discoloration may recur." "DO NOT use a power washer."
Industry associationAsphalt Roofing Manufacturers Association · GAF bulletin R-102
Even the companies that sell soft-wash bleach admit rinsing doesn't neutralize it, and the leaf burn shows up after you've left.
"Water dilutes SH but does not neutralize it." … "the lingering chemical that causes leaf burn hours after you leave the property."
Soft-wash supplierJ. Racenstein, "How to protect plants when soft washing"
Standard compatibility charts rate aluminum "not recommended" for bleach. Think screen cages and gutters.
Bleach solutions — Aluminum: "D: Not recommended." Buna rubber: "D."
University reference chartTennessee State University chemical compatibility guide

The dog

Bleach on the fur gets licked off. Strong bleach can chemically burn a dog's mouth.
"Dermal exposure often leads to ingestion because animals will self-groom and lick the bleach off." "Concentrated or 'ultra' bleaches may be corrosive (cause chemical burns)." — "Ingestion of undiluted bleach can cause injury to the mouth and esophagus."
Veterinary poison centersPet Poison Helpline · ASPCA Animal Poison Control

Your lungs, your crew, your family

Household cleaners are the #2 reason people call poison control, and bleach is the biggest one for kids.
2022: "2,064,875 human exposures"; top categories "analgesics (11.5%), household cleaning substances (7.23%)." Among children under 6, bleach was 24.7% of all cleaning-product calls — the largest single product.
Peer-reviewed2022 National Poison Data System annual report · Pacini et al., 2023
Chlorine has a 15-minute safety ceiling of half a part per million.
NIOSH REL: "C 0.5 ppm [15-minute]." Symptoms: "Burning of eyes, nose, mouth… cough, choking… pulmonary edema."
GovernmentNIOSH Pocket Guide: Chlorine
People who use bleach for a living get asthma more often.
New-onset asthma associated with "bleach/quaternary compounds (OR, 1.91)." Respiratory symptoms in cleaners "associated with… chlorine bleach."
Peer-reviewedCarson et al., 2024 (CDC) · Zock et al., 2010 review
Bleach meeting an acid makes chlorine gas. The CDC counted 409 poisonings from it in a single year.
"409 cases of chlorine exposure in 1990 from acid mixtures with hypochlorite"; "128 required treatment in health-care facilities."
GovernmentCDC Morbidity and Mortality Weekly Report

Bleach in a Florida summer

At 95°F, bleach loses half its strength in 48 days. 12.5% is about 10% within a week of being made.
"At 95 °F / 35 °C the half-life is 48 days." … "a product made at 12.5% available chlorine will have decomposed to circa 10% within a week."
Bleach manufacturersOrenda technical note · Solenis, "The stability of chlorine bleach solutions"
Hot bleach in a poly tank makes oxygen, and the tank maker says that can rupture the tank.
"Extensive oxygen buildup can cause a tank to rupture if left unchecked and unmitigated." "bleach solutions are best kept at temperatures beneath 60°F (15°C)."
Tank manufacturerNTO Tank

The law on wash water

Right here in Florida: no power-washing wash water in the storm drain, up to $5,000 a day.
"No pollutants of any kind, including wash water from power washing activities may be discharged" … "fined up to $5,000 per day."
Florida cityCity of Indian Rocks Beach, FL (2024) · City of Miami Springs, FL
Lee County, Naples and Estero: anything but rain going into a ditch, canal or storm drain is an "illicit discharge."
Lee County: "The discharge of any pollutant into a waterway, or a system connected to that waterway, is called an illicit discharge." Naples: prohibited discharges include "process wastewater, and wash water." Estero: violators pay "a fine to cover administrative and remediation costs."
Florida local governmentLee County Natural Resources · City of Naples · Village of Estero Ordinance 2020-08
St. Petersburg: never drain chlorinated water to the street, fines up to $10,000 a day. Florida DEP: chlorine is toxic to aquatic life even at low levels.
"Never drain chlorinated water to the street or stormdrain." "Violators of the stormwater ordinance may be fined up to $10,000 per day." — "Even at low levels, chlorine can be toxic to aquatic life."
Florida city / stateCity of St. Petersburg · Florida DEP stormwater BMPs
A roof-cleaning company's bleach mix reached a creek, killed 318 trout, and cost it an $11,000 penalty.
"discharge of a roof de-mossing mixture containing bleach into Reay Creek" … "administrative penalty of $11,000."
News, quoting the regulatorCHEK News (British Columbia)
California cities say the same, name bleach specifically, and fine up to $10,000 a day.
"It is illegal to discharge polluted wash water from pressure washing" … "up to $10,000 per day per incident." Sacramento: avoid cleaners with "hydrofluoric acid, muriatic acid, sodium hydroxide, bleach, etc."
CityCity of San Diego · Sacramento County

These rules cover all wash water, ours included. The difference is what's in it when it gets there.

What peroxide becomes

Hydrogen peroxide breaks down into water and oxygen. The EPA says so.
Peroxy compounds "degrade rapidly in air to form oxygen and water." "Readily biodegradable (i.e., < 10 days)."
GovernmentEPA peroxy compounds fact sheet · Massachusetts DEP review
Below 8%, peroxide isn't even a regulated hazardous material. Our working mix is about 7%.
"Less than 8% concentration H2O2 is considered a non-hazardous substance." (The 35% concentrate is a corrosive oxidizer and is handled like one.)
ReferenceUSU / NASA Peroxide Handbook
The EPA says risks to wildlife are minimal; the CDC says it doesn't build up in the food chain.
"Risks to wildlife are considered minimal." — "Hydrogen peroxide breaks down rapidly in water." "Hydrogen peroxide does not accumulate in the food chain."
GovernmentEPA peroxy compounds fact sheet · CDC / ATSDR ToxFAQs
Hydrogen peroxide is allowed on certified-organic farms and on restaurant food-contact surfaces. Bleach is not the only thing that kills mold.
USDA organic rule, 7 CFR 205.601: "Hydrogen peroxide." USDA review: "There are no known cases of hydrogen peroxide causing environmental contamination." FDA, 21 CFR 178.1010: "An aqueous solution of hydrogen peroxide … may be used on food-contact surfaces in public eating places."
Federal regulation7 CFR 205.601 · USDA NOSB review · 21 CFR 178.1010

What the homeowner wants

Half of shoppers put "safe around kids and pets" first. Offering the bleach-free option is how you win that half.
"safe for use around kids and pets (51%)"; "made with non-toxic or chemical free ingredients (41%)."
Consumer survey, 2025Numerator (household products survey)

What we won't tell you, because the evidence isn't there

  • That bleach voids your shingle warranty. No manufacturer says that. ARMA, GAF and Owens Corning still list a bleach-and-water solution and say to check with the shingle maker before using any other cleaner, including ours.
  • That our clean lasts longer as a proven fact. It's what we see on our own jobs; nobody has run the study.
  • That peroxide is harmless to plants or fish. It's far gentler and it breaks down to water and oxygen, but a fresh 7% mix will still scorch a leaf it sits on. Rinse the plants.
